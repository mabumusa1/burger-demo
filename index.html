The smash burger rules nobody follows

By J. Reyes · 6 min read

🍔

A smash burger is not a thin hamburger. It is a different cooking method that happens to use the same ingredients, and almost everything that goes wrong with one goes wrong in the first fifteen seconds.

1. The ball goes on the griddle cold

Room-temperature beef spreads before it sears. You want the fat still firm when it hits the steel, so the outside can brown while the inside is barely warm. Weigh out 85 gram balls, keep them on a tray in the fridge, and take out only what fits on the surface.

2. Smash once, within ten seconds

The crust is a Maillard reaction and it needs contact. Press hard, press flat, and press only once. A second press after the proteins have set tears the crust you just built and squeezes out the fat that was going to carry the flavour.

If you can hear it from the next room, the griddle is hot enough.

3. Salt after the smash, not before

Salt draws moisture to the surface. Salt the ball early and you steam the first thirty seconds away. Salt it flat, on the griddle, and the crust is already forming underneath.

4. The cheese goes on while the patty is still on the heat

Cheese added after the flip, on a patty already resting, never melts properly. It sits there looking like a slice of cheese on a burger, which is what it is. Put it on while the second side is still cooking and cover it for twenty seconds.

What to do with the bun

Toast it in the beef fat. Not butter, not a separate pan. The fat left behind after the patties come off is the best thing on the griddle and most people wipe it away.
